The origin of the name

WhyFrequentSuspicion?

FrequentSuspicion comes directly from PTSD—and from the suspiciousness of all things and all people that followed me home.

The battlefield trained my mind and body to search constantly for danger: to read a room before entering it, notice every exit, question every silence, measure every change in tone, study every motive, and assume that what looked safe might not be safe at all.

That vigilance helped me survive where danger was real. But the nervous system does not always understand when the war is over. The scanning continued. Suspicion became frequent because threat felt possible everywhere—in ordinary places, familiar voices, relationships, churches, kindness, and even peace itself.

“The battlefield exposed my heart. It didn’t write my story.”

The name does not celebrate distrust as wisdom. It names the condition honestly. It acknowledges the distance between discernment and fear, between genuine danger and a body that still expects it, and between surviving the past and learning how to live in the present.

FrequentSuspicion is the sound of bringing that hypervigilance before God—asking Him to separate truth from fear, conviction from shame, discernment from paranoia, and protection from isolation.

01

No edited testimony

I refuse to make myself the hero.

I write as a husband, a father of two daughters, and a veteran living with the aftermath of combat and PTSD. Trauma explains part of my struggle, but it cannot excuse the harm I caused. The songs name broken marriages, damaged trust, pride, secrecy, spiritual failure, church silence, wounded relationships, and choices I knew were wrong when I made them.

This is not confession for spectacle. It is the refusal to protect an image at the expense of truth. Some of the fire around my life was built and fed by my own hands. Repentance began when I stopped renaming it.

02

Grace with a backbone

Mercy and accountability belong together.

These songs also confront what happens when churches, families, and respected leaders protect comfort or reputation instead of people. Forgiveness is not denial. Mercy is not passivity. Biblical love does not ask the wounded to call evil peace.

FrequentSuspicion challenges spiritual language used to conceal coercion, excuse cruelty, soften responsibility, or silence those who came forward with the truth. Grace heals because truth sees.

03

The purpose

Turn the wreckage into a warning—and a witness.

I cannot rewrite yesterday. I can tell the truth about it. If one veteran recognizes that surviving is not the same as healing, if one father turns toward his family, if one husband refuses the passing flame, or if one church chooses courage over image, then the ruins can become something other than a grave.

The music exists for veterans carrying invisible battles, broken families, questioning believers, people wounded by churches, and anyone wondering whether God still enters places filled with ashes.
