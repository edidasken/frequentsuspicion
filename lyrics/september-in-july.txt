[Intro - 30 Bar Lead | No Vocals]

[Verse 1]
It was July in the desert, flags in the air, families laughing, lawn chairs everywhere. My daughter was close, the night felt kind, until the first blast shattered the back of my mind. One flash in the sky, and the years fell through, red turned to fire, and the smoke felt true. The crowd saw colors, I saw dust and flame, a date in September still calling my name. I tried to stay present, tried to breathe slow, but the past doesn't ask when it wants to show. It came through thunder, it came through light, turned Independence into another fight.

[Build]
I know this is today.
I know where I stand.
But some memories return
Before I can understand.

[Chorus]
September in July,
The sky split open wide.
Everybody saw the fireworks,
I saw the day that never died.

Freedom rang above me,
But the war rose up inside.
I was standing with the living,
With September in July.

[Verse 2]
Twenty minutes caught between here and then, trying to find my way back into my body again. My face full of tears, my chest locked tight, a grown man breaking in the public light. Nobody meant harm, nobody knew, how a celebration could become what I went through. How sound has memory, how light can deceive, how the past can return when you finally breathe. I wanted the beauty, wanted the sky, wanted one clean moment with my family nearby. But some wounds answer before the heart, and some old battles don't stay in the dark.

[Bridge]
I don't hate the country.
I don't hate the flame.
I don't hate the freedom
That still carries names.

But freedom has a shadow,
And some of us know why.
We came back from the battlefield
With smoke behind our eyes.

So let me cry if I need to.
Let me shake and still stand.
Let the night be complicated
In this wounded promised land.

[Final Chorus]
September in July,
The sky split open wide.
Everybody saw the fireworks,
I saw the day that never died.

Freedom rang above me,
But the war rose up inside.
I was standing with the living,
With September in July.

[Outro]
July held the fireworks, September held the wound, and God held the man who came apart beneath the moon. I'm still learning how to carry the beauty and the pain, how to honor what was lost without being trapped inside the same. The sky can hold a memory, the heart can hold a scar, and grace can still meet me exactly where we are.
